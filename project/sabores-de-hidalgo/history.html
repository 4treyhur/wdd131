History & Culture

How Indigenous roots, mining history and geography shaped the cuisine of Hidalgo.

Otomí and Nahua roots

Long before the Spanish arrival, Otomí and Nahua peoples cultivated corn, beans, chile, nopal and maguey in the region. Maguey in particular provided food (leaves, worms, flowers), drink (aguamiel and pulque) and cooking materials, and it remains central to dishes such as barbacoa, mixiote and ximbó.

Insects like escamoles and chinicuiles are also part of this Pre-Hispanic food tradition.

📷 Maguey field, Valle del Mezquital

Mining and the Cornish legacy

In the 19th century, miners from Cornwall, England arrived in Real del Monte to work the silver mines. They brought the pasty, which was adapted locally with chile, mole and other fillings. Pastes are now one of Hidalgo’s best-known foods and are celebrated in annual festivals.

Geography and ingredients

Hidalgo spans dry valleys, cool pine forests, mountain streams and tropical lowlands. This variety gives the state a wide pantry: maguey and nopal in the Valle del Mezquital, apples and trout in the highlands, and banana leaves, corn and coffee in the Huasteca.

📷 Map of Hidalgo regions

Culinary traditions

  • Pit cooking: underground ovens with maguey leaves for barbacoa and ximbó.
  • Steaming and wrapping: maguey membranes, banana leaves and corn husks.
  • Community meals: zacahuil and Sunday barbacoa are made to be shared.
